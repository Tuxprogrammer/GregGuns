# Planets and placement (D17, D38, D128, D302, D303)

## Grid

A second, sparse M8 grid dedicated to the complex: one cell per 64 x 64 chunks (1024 blocks), at most one attempt per cell, and only `siloComplexChancePercent` (default 25) of the attempts are kept, decided from the plan's seed. The grid keeps M8's spawn exclusion (no box within 8 chunks of chunk 0,0). It hashes cells like the BIG ship grid but with its own salt (`COMPLEX_SALT`), so a complex and the ordinary ship of the same cell never share a seed, and an ordinary structure whose box meets a complex that exists or rolls in its dimension is left out (8 blocks of margin), so no surface structure stands on a complex. The complex is 240 x 240 blocks and sits at a seeded offset in its cell, so it fits whatever the offset.

## Decision, once

The first populate window that overlaps a complex measures and records the decision in the dimension's `PlanLedger` (`StructureState`); later windows obey it. The decision runs these checks in order, and a failure is logged (`Silo complex skipped at dimension ...`) and recorded as rejected:

1. **Biome.** On the Overworld the biome types at the box center must include one of `siloComplexOverworldBiomes` (default plains, forest, sandy, savanna, coniferous, snowy, mesa and dry types; hills and mountains are not in it, because relief across 240 blocks is what the depth rule below cuts into). Other dimensions accept any biome. The world type must also be in `worldgen.structureWorldTypes`, so no complex generates on a flat preset.
2. **Terrain.** M8's land check (height spread of 8 or less over the columns the window can see, no water), so a planet whose terrain cannot host the layout (a dimension without solid ground) is skipped. Complexes skip M8's village block scan: the next step sees every village, built yet or not.
3. **Depth.** The recorded ground must be at least 30 so the level (24 blocks under the ground) sits above bedrock.
4. **Structure starts (D302).** Whether a village or temple will stand on the box plus 8 blocks, from the world's own start rule (below). If one will, the complex **sinks 10 to 20 blocks deeper** (`ComplexPlacer.sinkFor`, its own roll), cut to what keeps the floor at y 6 or higher; it is skipped (reason `village` or `temple`) only when it cannot sink 10 blocks. The surface pieces (shaft mouths, the escape trapdoor, radar huts) stay where the layout puts them: the shafts and the ladder are simply longer.

The decision records the ground, the **sink** and the **activity level**, rolled once with the weights in force (`Decision.sink`, `Decision.variant`; NBT `Sink` and `Variant`, D304). Every later window, and `/gregguns locate`, use the recorded values, so changing the activity weights or the village rules later never splits a complex. A ledger entry from before D304 has no level and rolls with the current weights.

A sunk complex logs `Silo complex at dimension 0 cell a,b (x,z) sinks N blocks deeper: village (start chunk cx,cz, pieces x0..x1 x z0..z1)`; a skip line names what was in the way the same way.

### Structure starts (D302)

Villages and temples are decided by a start rule that is a pure function of the world seed and the biome or noise source, so the complex can know where one will stand before any of its chunks exist, from any window, whatever loads first. `StructureStarts` is the seam; `VanillaStarts.forWorld` is the world adapter:

- **Finding the generators.** Any field of the dimension's chunk provider holding a `MapGenVillage` or `MapGenScatteredFeature`. No provider-specific names, so vanilla and Realistic World Gen both work; a provider with neither (Galacticraft planets, the Nether) has no starts. Spacing, separation and the village size come from the instance (RWG builds villages at spacing 24, separation 8; vanilla at 32 and 8).
- **Is it on.** The vanilla provider builds structures only with map features on. RWG builds villages when `ConfigRWG.generateVillages` is true (read by reflection) and ignores map features; it has no temples. Any other provider counts as on, which errs toward sinking.
- **Per candidate.** `StartGrid` gives each region's candidate chunk (`World.setRandomSeed` with salt 10387312 for villages, 14357617 for temples). Candidates within 128 blocks of the box (32 for temples) are checked for biomes on the world chunk manager (biome layers or RWG noise; no chunk is read), then the start is built as the generator would build it (`MapGenBase`'s per-chunk seed, one `nextInt`, then `MapGenVillage.Start` or `MapGenScatteredFeature.Start`). A village counts only when sizeable (more than two non-road pieces), as vanilla's own rule. Its piece box gets 16 blocks of margin (paths, fields, villagers), a temple's 8. A start that throws counts as present. Results are cached per start chunk.
- **Cost.** Once per complex decision: a few region candidates, then one biome check and one start build for each candidate in reach.

## Roguelike Dungeons keep out (D303)

Roguelike Dungeons (in the pack, Overworld only) writes a whole dungeon from one chunk's populate, levels at y 50 to 10, so a dungeon near a complex cuts through it. Dungeons yield to complexes. `RoguelikeGuard` swaps Roguelike Dungeons' registered world generator, at the same weight, for a keep-out one at the ICBM module's postInit, through **reflection only**: GregGuns does not compile against it (GPL-3), and every class and method it uses is looked up when it installs. The startup log says `Roguelike Dungeons keep-out: attached`, or warns that it could not attach and leaves Roguelike Dungeons unchanged. Switch: `compat.roguelikeKeepOutOfComplexes` (default true).

- A chunk that is not a dungeon spawn chunk (its own `canSpawnInChunk`) does nothing, as before. In a spawn chunk, the complex boxes within the dungeon's reach are collected (`StructurePopulator.complexBoxesNear`: a box the ledger placed, or an undecided one whose chance roll passes; a rejected one is free). No box: the original generator runs unchanged, with the same Random.
- With a box in reach the dungeon runs on a `KeepOutEditor`, a proxy over its own editor interface. While it looks for an entrance, a spot within the level range plus twice the scatter plus 16 (116 blocks with the pack config) of a box reads as ocean, so the dungeon tries its next spot; when all 50 tries fall near the complex there is no dungeon. Once it builds, every write inside a box plus 8 blocks, at any Y, is refused (its chest and spawner code checks the refusal before touching the tile), its tile lookups there return nothing, and its column fills and spiral stair steps that would reach the box are skipped. One line logs each dungeon that was moved or clipped.
- **Order.** A dungeon before the complex: its writes in the box are refused, the ground stays natural, and the complex writes normally. A complex before the dungeon: its blocks are never touched.
- **Known limit.** Other mods' small surface pieces (Witchery huts, Thaumcraft nodes, LootGames rooms, Forestry hives) have no start rule to read and can still meet a complex; none is large.
The level floor is the lower of the recorded ground and the provider's average ground level, minus 24, and never below y 6 (`ComplexPlacer.floorY`), then the recorded sink lower (D302), so a plan recorded on a rise still lies under the lower land around it. The tallest sections (the hub and the silo gallery, 12 layers) end 12 blocks under the recorded ground. Each window also reads its own columns: a column whose ground (the sea floor under water) lies less than 14 blocks above the floor is cut off, with its marks and contents, so no room breaks the surface or sits under water. A cut-off column keeps its natural terrain, so where its ground lies below the open cells of a written column beside it, those open cells are written as the section's wall: a hall never opens sideways onto a lake or valley. A neighbor one column past the window is read too, since it still lies in a chunk the populate call has loaded (`HeightProbe.seaFloorYBeside`, D247). Silo shafts and the escape ladder rise to the ground height read in their own window, so uneven ground is fine. The cell behind the escape ladder is written as stone from the level to the surface, so the ladder keeps something to cling to where a cave crosses the shaft.

## A complex that already exists

A plan the ledger holds as placed is written whatever the current roll or flags say, so lowering `siloComplexChancePercent`, removing a dimension from `siloComplexDimensions` or excluding a world type mid-world never cuts a complex off; only new decisions stop. A plan the ledger rejected is never written. The frontier case: turning ICBM on in a world that already generated chunks gives complexes whose first populate window was explored earlier no ledger entry and no blocks there, so a complex at the edge of the explored area appears as the half that is generated later. Windows already generated stay as they are.

## Never cascading

The plan and its raster are pure functions of the plan seed, so every window writes only its own columns through the clipped sink. A complex covers about 225 windows; none of them reads or writes another chunk. The section styles are pure too (the plan seed and the section id, the dimension key and the activity level), and so is the regrowth of a plan that misses its targets (D230). The whole window write runs with GT's machine-update search off for the thread (D205). Unit tests prove it with the cascade harness pattern (writes outside the window, rejected writes, foreign chunk requests, duplicates, order independence) and the `icbm.complex.places` scenario proves it in a live world.

**Cost (D236).** The I6b complex writes about three times the I6 blocks. `icbm.complex.places` and `icbm.complex.levels` note the blocks and the wall time per window that writes. When every window over a complex has run, the placer logs one debug line for it: windows, blocks written, columns cut off, and the last window's refused writes. A complex that writes nothing in a pack therefore shows its numbers in the log.

## Dimensions (D38)

The flag list is `siloComplexDimensions`, by the dimension's key (`DimensionKey`), compared **case-insensitively**. A Galacticraft planet's key is its celestial body's unlocalized name (`WorldProviderSpace.getCelestialBody().getName()`: "moon", "mars", "iojupiter", "ganymed", "barnarda2", "vega1"), the way GalacticGreg keys them, because `getDimensionName()` is the localized name ("Io", "Ganymede", "Barnarda C", "Vega B"), which differs from the table and by language. Any other dimension keeps its provider name ("Overworld"). Default: Overworld and every planet of the table. A dimension absent from the list gets no complex. **Galacticraft drops Forge's world generators on every planet** (except a short whitelist of other mods), so the generator would never run there. `Ross128ProviderHook` therefore wraps the chunk provider of Ross128b and Ross128ba and of every non-Overworld world where the complex is enabled (`StructureGenerator.complexWanted`), and runs the generator right after the provider's own populate, in the same window; the call that comes second is skipped. The generator runs in a dimension when either the ordinary structures are allowed there or the complex is, and then only places what is allowed.

A dimension the config lists that the table lacks (`tcetie` in an older config, or another mod's world) gets no complexes, and the log names it once; it is never generated at a fallback ceiling (D249).

## Data stick (nearest other complex)

The comms data stick holds the real block coordinates (x, z of the assembly bay) of the nearest other complex. `NeighborComplex.nearest` asks the placement grid (`StructurePopulator.complexPlanAt`) which of the cells within 3 of this one roll a complex at the configured chance, takes the anchor of each, and picks the nearest. It reads only the seed, the dimension and the grid: no world, no chunk, so it can never load or generate one. It cannot know that terrain will later reject that neighbor (skip-and-log), so a stick may name a place with no complex. No complex in range: no stick.

## Locating structures

`/gregguns locate <kind> [radius_cells] [generate]` is an op command (permission level 2, the console included) on every server where structures generate. The kind is `complex` (the silo complex, alias `silo_complex`), a structure id (`military_base`, alias `camp`; `big_bunker`, `factory_small`, `train_station`, `gas_station`, `house_medium`, `alien_bug_nest`, `aircraft_carrier`, `submarine`) or `any`. It lists the sites within `radius_cells` (1 to 16 cells of 1,024 blocks, default 3) of the sender in the sender's dimension, nearest footprint center first, up to eight rows. `StructureLocator` takes them from the placement grids and reads no world, so it loads no chunk: the complex from `StructurePopulator.complexGridPlanAt` plus its chance roll (`icbm.siloComplexChancePercent`), every other structure from the three size-class `StructureGrid`s (one weighted draw per cell, so the chance is the spacing and the pool weights; a site a silo complex covers is never drawn and is not listed). Each row names the structure, the cell, the footprint center, the distance and what the dimension's placement ledger (`StructureState`, saved with the world; every structure's decision goes through it) holds: built (with its arrival point; a complex also shows its recorded activity level and how much deeper it was sunk), skipped (with the recorded reason: biome, low ground, water or no ground, too steep, village, temple, not water, too shallow) or not generated yet (terrain unknown). A complex the ledger built stays listed after the chance is lowered; a skipped one is listed while its cell still rolls. Each new decision of an ordinary structure is also logged (`Structure <key>: placed|skipped`); complexes keep their own skip line.

`generate` takes the nearest sites that have no record (at most five) and decides them: it loads the 2 x 2 chunks around each footprint's center, one chunk per server tick, which populates the window that decides the plan (the first populate of an overlapping window decides it, so the verdict is the center window's, as in play); a half-generated window gets the ring of chunks around. The command prints a line per site as it finishes and a total. The chunks it loaded are queued for unloading; the ledger and the blocks the populate wrote are saved, and the rest of a large structure builds as its chunks generate. One run at a time.

A built row is clickable and runs `/gregguns locate tp <id> <small|medium|big> <cellX> <cellZ>`, which teleports the player onto the surface at the arrival point (the complex's escape ladder, the flush trapdoor; the footprint center for the rest), loading its chunk first so the height is the real one. The scenario `icbm.complex.locate` checks the complex listing against the placement grid and the populate path, a run of `generate` against the ledger, the listing again afterwards, and a `any` listing and run against the ordinary grids.

### Rebuilding a complex (D301)

`/gregguns locate rebuild <cellX> <cellZ>` (ops, the same permission as `locate`) clears a built silo complex's box in the sender's dimension and writes it again with the code the server runs now, so an existing world shows the current rooms. The cell is the one the listing prints. A cell with no built complex in the ledger is refused. It keeps the ledger entry as it is, never re-deciding: the activity level and the depth recorded when the complex was first decided carry over. It runs through `LocateGeneration`'s tick driver, one step per server tick, and shares its one-run-at-a-time rule (`ComplexRebuild`):

1. **Clear,** one chunk per tick over every chunk the 240 x 240 box touches. In each column of the box, from the lowest level floor any complex has (`ComplexPlacer.MIN_FLOOR`, above the bedrock) up to the column's natural surface, it puts back the blocks the dimension's terrain generator makes there (`currentChunkProvider.provideChunk`, the terrain without populate: caves and water included, ores and other populate features not). Whatever an older complex built under the ground is gone. Over the surface, up to 16 blocks above it, only the complex's own pieces go: the radar huts' casings (Works, Gantry and Radar Casing), a GT machine standing on a Radar Casing, and silo concrete within 3 blocks of the ground. A container is emptied before its block is replaced, so nothing spills. Then every dropped item, item frame and creature under the surface of the box is removed, and a player under it is moved up onto it.
2. **Write,** one populate window per tick over every window that overlaps the box: the complex writer runs exactly as chunk populate does (the same clipped sink, height probe and machine hook), with the ledger's decision. The four chunks of each window are loaded first.

A progress line every 32 chunks or windows, one when the clear ends (with the number of blocks it changed) and one at the end (with the activity level). The chunks the run loaded are queued for unloading. The scenario `icbm.complex.rebuild` builds a complex, leaves stray blocks and an item in it and a block just outside it, rebuilds it, and checks what is gone, what stayed, the ledger, the hall's signs and a silo's five-wide front.

## Per-dimension checks

`icbm.complex.dim.<overworld|moon|mars|ross128b|ross128ba>` force the chance to 100 percent, pick cells that were never loaded, load the 17 x 17 chunk region through the dimension's own provider and check: the plan was decided; no dropped write, no PopulateGuard violation; no chunk loaded beyond the request plus the populate margin; trapdoor flush and ladder facing; shaft rings meet the terrain and an open bore sees the sky; radar huts, doorway, and the radar on each roof (its controller stands unformed and forms once an Energy Hatch is added); the stick's coordinates; the planned deep posts; the armory frames each hold a gun and a valid wall. A cell the terrain rejects is noted and the next is tried. `icbm.complex.skips` runs water, too-low ground, a ridge repeated in every window and an off-whitelist biome through the harness and checks one logged reason each, nothing written, and flat ground placed; its village cases decide a complex under a village start from opposite corner windows and check the same sunk floor both times, and a skip when it cannot sink. `icbm.complex.village_starts` finds real village starts in the dev world and checks the decision sinks under one and not elsewhere, with no chunk loaded beyond the populate margin. `icbm.complex.roguelike_guard` checks the guard attached when Roguelike Dungeons is loaded (skipped otherwise). Run in the dev environment: Overworld, Moon, Mars, Ross128b and Ross128ba (Galacticraft, BartWorks). GalaxySpace and Amun-Ra are not in the dev environment, so their planets are untested; the table and the hook are by name and apply unchanged. Known limit: the terrain check samples only the first window that reaches the complex; the per-column cut above handles relief seen later. The scenarios run the dimensions the dev environment has (Overworld, Moon, Mars, Ross128b, Ross128ba); the planets past tier 2 (GalaxySpace, Amun-Ra) are not in it, so `icbm.complex.dim.*` for them runs in the alpha gate's batched pass on the real pack, and `PlanetNamesTest` checks all 33 keys and ceilings meanwhile (D207). Each dimension scenario queues the chunks it loaded, and a planet its whole world, for unload when it ends, so one prefix run over every planet does not hold them all at once (D239). While a player is in the dimension it releases nothing (D250).

## Config

`icbm.siloComplexEnabled`, `siloComplexDimensions`, `siloComplexChancePercent`, `siloComplexOverworldBiomes`, `siloComplexRoss128Weights` (nine weights), `siloComplexActivityWeights` (default 30, 40, 30; D304); `compat.roguelikeKeepOutOfComplexes`. The complex also needs `modules.worldgen` and `worldgen.spawnStructures`.
