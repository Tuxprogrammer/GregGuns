# 02-explosives: explosives and blasts

## Overview

Every explosive in ICBM-Classic `prod/1.12` @ `9c1d985a`:
- **Part a:** the registry and tiers, the shared blast engine (threaded blasts, ray-casting, edit budgets), the nuclear, thermobaric and antimatter blasts, and the explosive block.
- **Part b:** each individual blast type, gases and status effects, radioactive blocks and mutation, and the effect entities (flying blocks, fragments, smoke, beams).
- **Part c:** red matter, with its performance budget.

The EMP is in [05-detection-defense](../05-detection-defense/emp-tower.md). Missiles, carts and grenades that carry these explosives are in [03-missiles](../README.md#areas).

## Key facts

### Part a: the engine

- Tier is visual. The only thing it does is choose content forms: tiers 1 to 4 get a block, cart and missile; **only tier 1 gets a grenade**; tier NONE gets nothing (hypersonic, missile module, rot, mutation).
- The explosive identity is an integer in the item damage value. IDs 0 to 26 are fixed (12 and 14 unused); `colors` and `smoke` take the next free IDs, which on a fresh registry are 12 and 14.
- All legacy blasts share one lifecycle. Ticking blasts are driven by an `EntityExplosion`. Nuclear, thermobaric and antimatter are not ticking: they have no entity, are not saved, and run on **one** worker thread by default.
- Nuclear and thermobaric are 50,000 and 18,000 energy rays. Every ray pays 1.125 per half block even in air, so a nuclear blast with `scale` 50 and `energy` 80 reaches at most about 36 blocks. Stone costs about 4.25 per block, obsidian stops everything.
- Blocks are removed with `onBlockExploded` (flag 3), with no drops. Explosive blocks and TNT chain. Removal is spread over ticks by an edit queue (35 ms budget, nearest first), and because the queue handler runs on both tick phases the real budget is 70 ms.
- The post-blast effects (second damage wave, rot, mutation, second sound) fire when the crater is *queued*, not when it is finished.
- Entity damage is `(s^2 + s) / 2 x 8 x power + 1` with `s = (1 - d) x shielding`. With the default power 80,000 any entity within 50 m that is not fully enclosed dies.
- Antimatter: radius 55, damage 2^31 - 1, deletes bedrock by default, kills all red matter within a 221-block box, and with the default config does nothing else when it finds one.
- The explosive block stores nothing in metadata but its facing. The explosive is in the tile's stack. Triggers: redstone, flint and steel or fire charge, a burning arrow, any explosion (chain reaction), and adjacent fire or lava at placement. Breaching skips the entity and fires instantly into the block it is placed on.
- Capabilities (`CapabilityBlast*`), the registry events, blockstates, the `explosive` block property, datafixers and Forge `@Config` are *1.12-only mechanisms*.

### Part b: blast types and effect entities

- Every blast's radius is `max(3, size)`. The registry's per-blast number is the size, and for the shrapnel family it is the fragment count per axis (total entities = square).
- The TNT family is vanilla's cube-surface ray cast with 1,352 rays. Repulsive and attractive are the same class with a fixed 12-block push whose velocity (up to 12 blocks/tick) ignores the config scale. The "destroy items" flag is dead for them.
- Breaching is a set of straight energy lines. A block that is too tough for the remaining energy is skipped without stopping the line, so a tunnel can pass through intact obsidian.
- The sonic blast must finish its block search by tick 12 of a 16-tick blast or it removes nothing, and it can only reach about 0.9 of its radius.
- The beam blasts take about 6 seconds in stages and then rewrite the whole sphere in one tick; their flying-block step is half implemented (the gravity-restore set is never filled).
- The anti-gravitational blast lifts one block per tick for two minutes; its entity-lift box is built with the wrong coordinates, so it covers a wide band of X.
- Gas clouds are a flood fill of block cells that grows in radius steps, and they sink into any passage that is not a full-face solid. One packet per cell goes to clients. Damage grows with time spent inside. Contagious gas does not spread between entities; it is Poison IV plus mutation.
- Protective armor is a sum of per-item ratings from config; the defaults name `atomicscience` hazmat items, so a GTNH pack must supply its own list. The affected rule is "rating below the threshold, or below a random roll".
- Radioactive blocks hurt for 2 damage at about 85% per run within a 5-block cube and decay on average in about 25 minutes. They begin ticking only after their first random tick. Rot swaps 20% of stone and dirt in an 80-block sphere, so a nuclear blast leaves hundreds of thousands of them.
- Flying blocks re-place themselves through the item-placement path and lose tile entity data (chests arrive empty); with no impact they place themselves wherever the timer expires, even in mid-air; hitting an entity drops an item every tick.
- A primed explosive stores its fuse as a byte, so a fuse above 127 (antimatter's 300) saved mid-countdown detonates on load. Its blast is also centered half a block off the entity on all three axes.
- Nothing in these classes checks whether a chunk is loaded before reading blocks, and several effects set entity motion directly without the sync flag a player needs.
- Capabilities, `EntityEntryBuilder`, `DataParameter`, block states, the config annotations and the registry events are *1.12-only mechanisms*; the sheets describe the behavior they produce.

### Part c: red matter

- Red matter is an **entity with no end**: starvation only works below size 1, nothing caps its lifetime, and after the ball is clear it keeps tracing 501 rays per tick and scanning a cube 284 blocks wide at default max, forever.
- Hard per-tick ceilings at defaults: **501 ray traces, 101 block removals** (plus up to 6 uncounted ice edits per removed water block), **one entity scan** of edge `4 x size`, about 20 debris entities per tick on average at full speed.
- Growth is by scan radius: +1 per empty cycle. From nothing, size 1 reaches the cap (R = 70, size 71) in about **5,500 ticks (4.6 minutes)**. A cycle at R = 70 is 119,286 ray targets. Clearing a solid ball of radius 70 takes at least 14,800 ticks (12.4 minutes) at the 101-edit cap.
- Size is rewritten to `R + 1` at each growth step, so merged mass and eaten debris do not persist, and the size settles one above the configured max.
- A reloaded red matter comes back at size 70 and scans at R = 70 at once (NBT bug). Avoid saving and loading during a test.
- The sound is the cheapest thing to cut: the 1.01 s `redmatter` clip is started every tick (20 overlapping copies), pitch drawn from a range that is partly negative. `redmatter_audio_enabled` turns it off.
- Everything is removed with no drops, no event and no protection check; tile contents are lost.
- Capabilities, data parameters, the entity entry and GLU rendering are *1.12-only mechanisms*.

## Known limits

### Part a: the engine

- No code was run. Every number (ray counts, energy reach, damage tables, edit counts, timings) comes from reading the source and arithmetic on the config defaults.
- The exact on-disk spelling of Forge config keys for fields without `@Config.Name` (the fuse times, nuclear, thermobaric) was not verified in-game; the Java field names are given.
- ICBM-02-22 (wrong item dropped when an explosive block is mined) is inferred from the block state having no explosive stored; it was not confirmed in-game and no override of `getDrops` was found.
- The burning-arrow trigger depends on whether a stuck arrow reaches `onEntityCollidedWithBlock`; not verified.
- Whether the remote detonator, laser detonator and defuser act on the block, the entity or the capability was not checked here (see `05-detection-defense`).
- The worker-thread safety claims (reading chunks off-thread) follow the code's own TODO and vanilla 1.12 behavior; no crash was reproduced.
- Sounds and textures were not seen or heard. Sound durations and sample rates were not measured.
- Forge `ExplosionEvent.Start` cancels the blast but a mod that cancels it receives `EXPLOSION_CANCELED` through the action status; how callers handle that status is in `01-core` and part b.

### Part b: blast types and effect entities

- No code was run. All numbers, probabilities and timings come from reading the source; the vanilla mechanics quoted (poison rate and floor, invulnerability frames, `World.getBlockState` loading chunks, player motion sync) are from memory of 1.12 behavior and are marked in the text where they matter. ICBM-02-45, 02-53, 02-71 and 02-75 are the least certain.
- The blast engine (`Blast` base, `BlastThreaded`, `WorkerThreadManager`, `BlockEditHandler`) is part a and was read only to understand what these classes call. Statements about completion, the active-blast list and block-edit queuing are consistent with those files but part a is the authority.
- `EntityProjectile` is shared with the missile area. Its in-ground and impact rules are summarized here only as far as the flying block depends on them.
- The defaults for fuse times and for the explosives not in this part are in part a.
- Sounds and textures could not be heard or seen; descriptions come from names and use.
- The vanilla blocks and effects these blasts produce (magma, snow layers, zombie villagers, potion rates) are 1.12 content; a GTNH 1.7.10 rebuild must check each one exists there (the magma block and the separate zombie villager entity do not). The rebuild notes belong to `docs/gregguns-icbm-spec`.
- Observed outside this part (for part a): `Blast.getBlastSource()` (`content/blast/Blast.java:470-473`) returns the result of calling itself, which would overflow the stack if anything called it. Nothing in the files read here does.

### Part c: red matter

- No code was run and nothing was profiled. All counts, ramp times and memory sizes come from reading the source and arithmetic on the config defaults. Object sizes (about 24 bytes per position and per list node) are estimates for a typical 64-bit JVM.
- The vanilla 1.12 behaviors that the performance numbers lean on were taken from knowledge of the game and not checked against a decompile: the block ray trace caps at about 200 steps and does not check whether chunks are loaded; block-change packet batching; the sound packet range of 16 x volume; the client clamp of pitch to 0.5 to 2; the 28-channel sound limit; the ender dragon ignoring damage that does not come through its parts; the void kill at y below -64.
- The debris lifetime (10 to 20 ticks) is an estimate from the pull acceleration, not a simulation. `EntityProjectile` handles collision for debris; its full behavior is in `03-missiles` and `effect-entities.md`.
- Whether `/icbm blast trigger` forwards the size to the action's `setValue` was inferred from the call site and the override; not traced in `01-core`.
- Whether `/icbm` has a remove sub-command that reaches `BlastRedmatterWrapper.clearBlast` was not checked.
- The sounds and textures were not seen or heard. The renderer was read, not run; the descriptions of the disk, sphere and beams come from the draw calls.
- Pull velocities were computed assuming the default config; real entity motion also includes vanilla drag, which differs per entity type.
