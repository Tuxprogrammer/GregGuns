## Key facts

- Every blast's radius is `max(3, size)`. The registry's per-blast number is the size, and for the shrapnel family it is the fragment count per axis (total entities = square).
- The TNT family is vanilla's cube-surface ray cast with 1,352 rays. Repulsive and attractive are the same class with a fixed 12-block push whose velocity (up to 12 blocks/tick) ignores the config scale. The "destroy items" flag is dead for them.
- Breaching is a set of straight energy lines. A block that is too tough for the remaining energy is skipped without stopping the line, so a tunnel can pass through intact obsidian.
- The sonic blast must finish its block search by tick 12 of a 16-tick blast or it removes nothing, and it can only reach about 0.9 of its radius.
- The beam blasts take about 6 seconds in stages and then rewrite the whole sphere in one tick; their flying-block step is half implemented (the gravity-restore set is never filled).
- The anti-gravitational blast lifts one block per tick for two minutes; its entity-lift box is built with the wrong coordinates, so it covers a wide band of X.
- Gas clouds are a flood fill of block cells that grows in radius steps, and they sink into any passage that is not a full-face solid. One packet per cell goes to clients. Damage grows with time spent inside. Contagious gas does not spread between entities; it is Poison IV plus mutation.
- Protective armor is a sum of per-item ratings from config; the defaults name `atomicscience` hazmat items, so a GTNH pack must supply its own list. The affected rule is "rating below the threshold, or below a random roll".
- Radioactive blocks hurt for 2 damage at about 85% per run within a 5-block cube and decay on average in about 25 minutes. They begin ticking only after their first random tick. Rot swaps 20% of stone and dirt in an 80-block sphere, so a nuclear blast leaves hundreds of thousands of them.
- Flying blocks re-place themselves through the item-placement path and lose tile entity data (chests arrive empty); with no impact they place themselves wherever the timer expires, even in mid-air; hitting an entity drops an item every tick.
- A primed explosive stores its fuse as a byte, so a fuse above 127 (antimatter's 300) saved mid-countdown detonates on load. Its blast is also centered half a block off the entity on all three axes.
- Nothing in these classes checks whether a chunk is loaded before reading blocks, and several effects set entity motion directly without the sync flag a player needs.
- Capabilities, `EntityEntryBuilder`, `DataParameter`, block states, the config annotations and the registry events are *1.12-only mechanisms*; the sheets describe the behavior they produce.

## Known limits

- No code was run. All numbers, probabilities and timings come from reading the source; the vanilla mechanics quoted (poison rate and floor, invulnerability frames, `World.getBlockState` loading chunks, player motion sync) are from memory of 1.12 behavior and are marked in the text where they matter. ICBM-02-45, 02-53, 02-71 and 02-75 are the least certain.
- The blast engine (`Blast` base, `BlastThreaded`, `WorkerThreadManager`, `BlockEditHandler`) is part a and was read only to understand what these classes call. Statements about completion, the active-blast list and block-edit queuing are consistent with those files but part a is the authority.
- `EntityProjectile` is shared with the missile area. Its in-ground and impact rules are summarized here only as far as the flying block depends on them.
- The defaults for fuse times and for the explosives not in this part are in part a.
- Sounds and textures could not be heard or seen; descriptions come from names and use.
- The vanilla blocks and effects these blasts produce (magma, snow layers, zombie villagers, potion rates) are 1.12 content; a GTNH 1.7.10 rebuild must check each one exists there (the magma block and the separate zombie villager entity do not). The rebuild notes belong to `docs/gregguns-icbm-spec`.
- Observed outside this part (for part a): `Blast.getBlastSource()` (`content/blast/Blast.java:470-473`) returns the result of calling itself, which would overflow the stack if anything called it. Nothing in the files read here does.
